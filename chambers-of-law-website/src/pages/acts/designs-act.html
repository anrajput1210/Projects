The Designs Act, 2000

The legislation governing registration and protection of industrial designs in India.

Purpose and scope

The Designs Act, 2000 replaced the earlier Designs Act of 1911 and brought Indian design law in line with international standards under TRIPS. It governs the registration of the visual features of shape, configuration, pattern, and ornamentation applied to an article.

What it covers

The Act sets out the criteria for a registrable design (new or original, and significantly distinguishable from known designs), the classification system for articles, the registration procedure, the term of protection and renewal, and remedies for piracy of a registered design.

Why it matters to your business

For product-based businesses, a distinctive shape or surface pattern can be commercially as valuable as a brand name — the Designs Act is the tool for protecting that visual identity specifically, separate from trademark or patent protection.

This page summarises the purpose and scope of the Act for general information. It is not a substitute for the statutory text or for legal advice on a specific matter, and does not reproduce the Act's provisions verbatim.
