The Copyright Act, 1957

The legislation governing copyright protection for literary, artistic, musical, and other original works in India.

Purpose and scope

The Copyright Act, 1957 (as amended, notably in 2012 to address digital rights and performers' rights) governs how original works are protected, the rights authors and owners hold, and the remedies available for infringement. Protection arises automatically on creation — the Act does not require registration for a work to be protected.

What it covers

The Act defines the categories of protected work (literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works, cinematograph films, and sound recordings), the bundle of exclusive rights an owner holds (reproduction, distribution, adaptation, and public performance, among others), permitted exceptions such as fair dealing, and both civil and criminal remedies for infringement.

Why it matters to your business

Marketing copy, software, product photography, and training materials are all copyright works the moment they're created — understanding ownership rules (particularly for work created by employees or contractors) prevents disputes over who actually holds the rights.

This page summarises the purpose and scope of the Act for general information. It is not a substitute for the statutory text or for legal advice on a specific matter, and does not reproduce the Act's provisions verbatim.
