The Patents Act, 1970

The legislation governing what can be patented in India, and how patent rights are obtained and enforced.

Purpose and scope

The Patents Act, 1970 (as amended, most significantly in 2005 to introduce product patents in pharmaceuticals and align with TRIPS) governs the grant, examination, and enforcement of patents in India. It defines what qualifies as a patentable invention, the application and examination procedure, and the remedies available against infringement.

What it covers

Key provisions include the criteria for patentability (novelty, inventive step, industrial applicability), the categories of subject matter excluded from patenting under Section 3, procedures for pre- and post-grant opposition, compulsory licensing provisions, and the term of protection (20 years from filing).

Why it matters to your business

Understanding what the Act excludes from patentability — and how examiners apply those exclusions in practice — is often as important as understanding what qualifies, particularly for software-related and business-method inventions.

This page summarises the purpose and scope of the Act for general information. It is not a substitute for the statutory text or for legal advice on a specific matter, and does not reproduce the Act's provisions verbatim.
