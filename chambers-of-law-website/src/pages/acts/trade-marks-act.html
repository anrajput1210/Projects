The Trade Marks Act, 1999

The primary legislation governing trademark registration, protection, and enforcement in India.

Purpose and scope

The Trade Marks Act, 1999 replaced the earlier Trade and Merchandise Marks Act, 1958, and brought Indian trademark law in line with India's obligations under the TRIPS Agreement. It governs how trademarks are registered, the rights that registration confers, the process for opposing and cancelling registrations, and civil remedies for infringement.

What it covers

The Act sets out the classification of goods and services, the grounds on which a mark may be refused registration, procedures for examination, publication, and opposition, provisions for well-known marks and collective/certification marks, and the criminal and civil remedies available against infringement and counterfeiting.

Why it matters to your business

Every trademark filing, renewal, opposition, and enforcement action we handle for clients operates within the framework this Act establishes — understanding its structure helps clients see why certain filing decisions or litigation strategies make sense.
