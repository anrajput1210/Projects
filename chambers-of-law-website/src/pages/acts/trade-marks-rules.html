The Trade Marks Rules, 2017

The procedural rules that govern how trademark applications are actually filed and processed under the Trade Marks Act.

Purpose and scope

Where the Trade Marks Act, 1999 sets out the substantive law, the Trade Marks Rules, 2017 set out the procedure — the forms, fees, timelines, and formalities that applicants and the Registry must follow. The 2017 Rules replaced the earlier 2002 Rules, consolidating and simplifying the form structure and introducing reduced fees for individuals, startups, and small enterprises.

What it covers

The Rules prescribe the forms used at each stage (including Form TM-A for applications and Form TM-O for oppositions), filing fees and the concessions available to eligible applicants, timelines for responses at each procedural stage, and requirements for evidence, hearings, and expedited examination.

Why it matters to your business

Missing a procedural deadline set by the Rules — even where the underlying legal position is strong — can be fatal to an application or opposition. This is where careful docketing and attorney oversight earns its keep.

Some older reference materials cite the "Trade Mark Rules, 2015" — the current, controlling procedural rules are the Trade Marks Rules, 2017. This page reflects the rules currently in force, summarised for general information; it is not a substitute for legal advice on a specific filing.
