Trademark as Domain

Owning a trademark and owning the matching domain name are two separate things — until they collide.

Domains and trademarks are registered separately

Domain name registrars generally operate on a first-come, first-served basis and don't check trademark registers before issuing a domain. That means it's entirely possible for someone unconnected to your business to register a domain matching your brand name — sometimes in good faith, sometimes deliberately (known as cybersquatting).

What you can do about it

If you hold a registered trademark, you generally have a stronger basis to challenge a domain registered in bad faith through dispute resolution mechanisms such as the UDRP (for generic top-level domains) or INDRP (for .in domains), both of which allow a trademark owner to seek transfer of a domain registered or used in bad faith, without needing to go to court.

Practical steps

Registering your trademark strengthens your position in any domain dispute, and registering the matching domain (and common variants) early — ideally alongside your trademark filing — is the simplest way to avoid the issue altogether.

Domain dispute outcomes depend on the specific facts of registration and use. This page is a general overview, not advice on a specific dispute.
