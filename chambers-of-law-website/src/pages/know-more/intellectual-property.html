Intellectual Property

An overview of the legal tools available to protect what your business creates.

What is intellectual property?

Intellectual property (IP) refers to creations of the mind — brand names, inventions, creative works, and designs — that the law allows individuals and businesses to own and control, much like physical property. IP rights give the holder the ability to prevent others from using their creation without permission, and to license or sell that right to others.

The main categories

Trademarks

Protect brand identifiers — names, logos, and slogans used to distinguish goods and services. See About Trademarks.

Patents

Protect new, inventive, and industrially applicable inventions. See Patent.

Copyright

Protects original literary, artistic, musical, and software works. See About Copyright.

Industrial Designs

Protects the visual appearance of a product. See Industrial Design.

Why it matters

For most businesses, IP isn't a single filing — it's a portfolio that grows alongside the business, and one that competitors, investors, and acquirers will scrutinise. Getting the fundamentals right early, before a brand or invention gains value, is almost always cheaper than fixing a gap later.

This is a general overview of IP categories. Which protections apply to your situation depends on the specific facts of what you're seeking to protect.
