About Trademarks

A trademark is often a business's most valuable asset — here's what it protects and why registering it matters.

What is a trademark?

A trademark is any word, name, symbol, logo, sound, colour combination, or device used to identify and distinguish the goods or services of one business from another. In India, trademarks are governed by the Trade Marks Act, 1999, which allows registration of conventional marks like brand names and logos, as well as non-traditional marks such as sounds and packaging shapes in certain cases.

Why register a trademark?

Using a mark in commerce creates limited common-law rights, but registration gives the owner exclusive statutory rights to use the mark nationwide for the goods or services it covers, the ability to sue for infringement (not just passing-off), a legal presumption of ownership and validity, and the right to use the ® symbol. Registered marks can also be licensed, assigned, and used to build a portfolio that supports franchising or brand expansion.

What can — and can't — be registered

A mark must be capable of distinguishing your goods or services and must not be identical or deceptively similar to an existing registered or pending mark. Purely descriptive terms, generic names, and marks that are scandalous, deceptive, or identical to protected emblems generally cannot be registered without evidence of acquired distinctiveness.

How long does protection last?

A registered trademark in India is valid for 10 years from the date of application, and can be renewed indefinitely in further 10-year terms, provided renewal fees are paid and the mark continues to be used.

This page is a general overview for informational purposes and is not legal advice. Trademark eligibility and strategy depend on the specific facts of your mark and business.
