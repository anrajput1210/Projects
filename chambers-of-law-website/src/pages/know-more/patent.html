Patent

Protecting inventions that are genuinely new, inventive, and useful.

What's patentable

Under the Patents Act, 1970, an invention must be novel (not previously disclosed anywhere), involve an inventive step (not obvious to someone skilled in the field), and be capable of industrial application. Section 3 of the Act excludes certain subject matter from patentability, including mere discoveries, abstract theories, and — subject to specific conditions — certain computer programs and business methods.

Types of applications

A provisional application secures an early priority date while development continues, and must be followed by a complete specification within 12 months. A complete application can also be filed directly. For international protection, applicants often file under the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT), entering national phase in India and other target countries within the treaty's timelines.

The examination process

Applications are published 18 months after filing (or earlier on request), and examination begins only after a formal request for examination is filed. The examiner issues a First Examination Report raising any objections on novelty, inventive step, or clarity, which the applicant must respond to before a patent can be granted.

How long does protection last?

A granted patent is valid for 20 years from the filing date, subject to payment of annual renewal fees to keep it in force.

Patent eligibility is a detailed, fact-specific assessment. This page is a general overview and not an opinion on the patentability of any particular invention.
