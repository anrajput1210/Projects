Process of Registration

From clearance search to certificate — what actually happens during a trademark filing.

  1. Clearance search. We search the Registry database for identical or confusingly similar marks in your class before filing, to flag conflicts early.
  2. Application filing (Form TM-A). The application is filed with the mark's representation, applicant details, and specification of goods/services, along with the prescribed fee.
  3. Examination. A Registry examiner reviews the application for compliance and potential conflicts, and may issue an examination report raising objections that require a written response.
  4. Publication. Once accepted, the mark is published in the Trademark Journal, opening a four-month window during which third parties may oppose.
  5. Opposition period (if any). If opposed, the matter proceeds through counter-statement, evidence, and hearing stages — see our Trademark Opposition page.
  6. Registration. If unopposed or the opposition is resolved in your favour, the Registry issues a registration certificate, valid for 10 years from the application date.
  7. Renewal. The mark can be renewed indefinitely in further 10-year terms, provided renewal fees are paid before expiry.
Timelines depend on Registry workload and whether objections or oppositions arise. This is a general process overview, not a guarantee of any specific timeline.
