Geographical Indications

Protecting products whose qualities are tied to where they come from — like Darjeeling Tea or Basmati Rice.

What is a GI?

A Geographical Indication (GI) identifies goods as originating from a specific place, where a given quality, reputation, or characteristic is essentially attributable to that geographical origin. In India, GIs are protected under the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, and cover agricultural products, foodstuffs, handicrafts, and manufactured goods alike.

Who can apply?

Unlike trademarks, GIs are collective rights — an application is typically filed by an association of producers, a statutory body, or an organisation representing the interests of producers in the relevant region, rather than by a single individual or company.

How registration works

Applications are filed with the Geographical Indications Registry in Chennai and must establish the link between the product's characteristics and its geographical origin, often supported by historical, agronomic, or production-process evidence. Once registered, a GI is valid for 10 years and can be renewed indefinitely.

GI vs. trademark

A trademark identifies a single business as the source of goods; a GI identifies a region as the source, and any producer within that region meeting the defined standards may use it. The two regimes serve different purposes and are not interchangeable.

This is a general overview of GI protection. Eligibility and evidentiary requirements are assessed case by case.
