Trade Mark Watching Service

Registration isn't the finish line — an unwatched mark can be worn down by later conflicting filings you never opposed.

What a watch service does

A trademark watch service monitors new applications published in the Trademark Journal for marks that are identical or confusingly similar to yours, in classes relevant to your business. Because the opposition window is only four months from publication, catching a conflicting filing early is often the difference between a straightforward opposition and a costly later dispute.

Why it matters even after registration

A registered mark that goes unwatched can gradually be diluted by similar marks entering the market unopposed — weakening your ability to argue distinctiveness later, and making enforcement harder the longer a conflicting mark has coexisted. Watching is a low-cost way to protect a mark you've already invested in registering.

What we monitor

We track new filings across your relevant classes, phonetically and visually similar marks (not just identical ones), and applications from parties in your industry or geography where confusion risk is higher — flagging anything relevant well within the opposition window.

A watch service reduces the risk of missing a conflicting filing but cannot guarantee every relevant application will be identified.
